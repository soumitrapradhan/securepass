      Best practices

      Security Tips

      Six habits that do more for your account security than any complicated password rule.

      Use long and unique passwords

      Aim for 14+ characters. Length beats complexity: a long random password or passphrase is far harder to crack than a short "complex" one.

      Never reuse passwords

      When one site is breached, attackers try the same email and password everywhere else (credential stuffing). Every account needs its own password.

      Avoid predictable information

      Names, birthdays, phone numbers, pet names, and favourite teams are easy to find or guess. Keep personal details out of your passwords.

      Enable multi-factor authentication

      MFA adds a second proof of identity. Even if your password leaks, an attacker still cannot sign in. Prefer an authenticator app or a security key over SMS.

      Use a password manager

      A reputable password manager creates and remembers long random passwords for you, so you only need to remember one strong master passphrase.

      Change compromised passwords promptly

      If a service reports a breach or you spot suspicious activity, change that password immediately, and change it anywhere else it was used.

      Frequently asked questions

      Is the password I type sent or saved anywhere?

      No. Everything runs in your browser. The password is never uploaded, stored or logged. Click Reset to clear it, or just close the tab.

      How is the score calculated?

      SecurePass estimates entropy from the password's length and the types of characters it uses. It then lowers the estimate for common words, keyboard patterns (qwerty), sequences (abc, 123), repeats and years. The score runs from 0 to 100: 0–19 Very Weak, 20–39 Weak, 40–59 Medium, 60–79 Strong, 80–100 Very Strong.

      What is entropy?

      Entropy, measured in bits, shows how many guesses an attacker would need on average. Each extra bit doubles the work. It is only an estimate and does not cover every real attack.

      Why is my password weak even though it has capitals, numbers and symbols?

      Variety alone is not enough. Passwords like Summer2024! follow patterns attackers try first (a word, a year, a symbol at the end), so they score low. Length and randomness matter most.

      What length should I use?

      Use at least 12 characters, and 14–16 or more for important accounts such as email and banking.

      Is the generated password safe to use?

      Yes. It is created with your browser's cryptographically secure random generator (crypto.getRandomValues). It is created on your device and not saved, so copy it into a password manager straight away.

      Does a high score mean my password is completely secure?

      No. A strong password can still be stolen by phishing, malware or a data breach. Use a unique password for each account and turn on multi-factor authentication.

      The Copy button did not work. What now?

      Some browsers block copying on pages opened as local files. Select the generated password and press Ctrl + C, or open the site through its web link.
